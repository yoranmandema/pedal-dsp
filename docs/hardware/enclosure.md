# Enclosure, cutouts and thermal design — Raspberry Pi 5 pedal

Paper design, nothing built or measured yet. Every temperature below is a
**calculation from stated assumptions**, not a measurement. The sustained-load
card (`t_e77e98f1`, `docs/hardware/validation.md`) is what confirms or corrects it.

**Acceptance:** (a) someone can build the case from this doc; (b) the thermal
approach is stated with the reasoning for staying below 80 °C (§5).

Related: power supply `docs/hardware/power.md`, analogue front end
`docs/ANALOG-FRONTEND.md`, footswitches/expression `docs/design/us6.3-footswitches-and-expression-pedal.md`,
front-panel readout `docs/design/us6.5-display.md`.

## 0. Decisions (and two deviations to confirm)

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D1 | **Hammond 1590F** die-cast aluminium, 188 × 188 × 67 mm outer, unpainted or black. Chosen over the 1590BB/1590DD: the 7" 800×480 touchscreen module is ~165 × 100 mm and does not fit on a 120 mm-wide box. |
| D2 | **Closed die-cast box with a filtered slot pair in the lid** (intake + exhaust, §5.3) and a 40 mm fan. Not hermetically sealed: IP54-class (dust, splashes), no gasket. A fully sealed case cannot be held under 80 °C at the loads in `power.md`. |
| D3 | Cooling: **5 V 40 mm PWM fan on the Pi 5's own fan header**, ducted over a finned heatsink on the SoC and out of the exhaust slots. Plus metal-to-case thermal pad as a fan-failure fallback path (§5.4). |
| D4 | Power in: **9 V DC centre-negative 2.1 mm barrel** → 5 V buck as designed in `power.md`. |

**Deviations from the card comment** ("5 V 4 A switcher, 12 V fan"): `power.md` (landed) specifies a **5 A** buck, and there is **no 12 V rail** (only 9 V in, 5.05 V and 3V3). A 12 V fan would need a third regulator or a direct 9 V tap with a fan driver for no gain here. I used a **5 V fan** on the Pi's PWM header, which the OS already controls from SoC temperature. Swapping to a 12 V fan later changes only §6 parts and one wire. If the 12 V fan was intentional, say so.

## 1. Interior budget (1590F)

From the Hammond drawing: inside length 178.5 mm, inside width ~171 mm (wall draft up to 2°), inside height 62 mm with the lid on (the lid is 4 mm). Use **170 × 170 × 58 mm** usable.

## 5. Thermal design

### 5.1 Heat sources (from `power.md`)

| Source | Heat into the case |
|---|---|
| SoC (BCM2712), design peak with NAM and full chain | **9 W** (§2.1 gives 2.5 A × 5 V total Pi = 12.5 W; the SoC is ~70 %) |
| Rest of the Pi, USB devices, display, regulator losses (η 90 % at 13.6 W) | ≈ 6 W |
| Total dissipated in the box at peak | **≈ 15 W** |

The buck's 1.5 W loss and the display's 2.5 W are *not* on the heatsink; they heat the air in the box.

### 5.2 The chain, SoC to ambient air

```
 die -- Rjc 1.0 -- IHS -- TIM 0.4 -- heatsink 25x25x15 -- Rsa 1.5 (ducted, 4 m3/h) -- air
 (air also rises by P/(2 * rho * cp * flow) on the way through the sink)
```

* Rjc 1.0 K/W is an assumption (no published figure for the BCM2712 package; typical 15 mm-class flip-chip with IHS). Add a 50 % error band.
* TIM = pre-applied pad on the heatsink or paste, 0.3–0.5 K/W.
* Rsa 1.5 K/W for a 25 × 25 × 15 mm finned sink in a ducted 4 m³/h flow (a 40 mm fan at about 7 m³/h free air derated for the duct and the filter slot). Natural convection for the same sink is 6–9 K/W.
* Air mass flow 4 m³/h = 1.1 × 10⁻³ m³/s → heat capacity flow 1.28 W/K. With 15 W in the box, the exhaust air is 12 K above the intake; the die sees the **mean** of the air through the sink, ≈ +3.5 K at the 9 W heatsink load.

Die temperature = T_ambient + air rise + P × (Rjc + TIM + Rsa):

| Ambient (stage) | SoC load | Air rise | **Die temperature** | Margin to 80 °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 9 W | 3.5 | 25 + 3.5 + 9 × 2.9 = **54.6** | 25 K |
| 35 °C | 9 W | 3.5 | **64.6** | 15 K |
| **40 °C (design case: hot stage)** | 9 W | 3.5 | **69.6** | **10 K** |
| 45 °C (direct stage light) | 9 W | 3.5 | 74.6 | 5 K |
| 40 °C, flow only 2.5 m³/h (dirty filter, tired fan) | 9 W | 5.6 | 71.7 | 8 K |
| 40 °C, heatsink only 2.0 K/W | 9 W | 3.5 | 74.1 | 6 K |

**Why it should stay under 80 °C:** at the design point (40 °C venue, 9 W peak, which is above the typical 5–6 W of a NAM chain) the die is calculated at ≈ 70 °C. 10 K of margin is larger than the combined uncertainty on Rjc (±3 K) and a 40 % loss of airflow (+2 K). The fan curve in §5.5 reaches full speed at 72 °C, before the 80 °C soft limit. The health monitor (`t_3f42020c`) logs temperature and `get_throttled`, so a miss is detected rather than assumed.

### 5.3 Airflow path (nothing blocks it)

```
   lid (outside, floor)                     lid
  intake slots x15..45  -->  FAN 40 mm  -->  3D-printed duct -->  Pi heatsink -->  exhaust slots x162..182
   (filter foam 5 mm)         at (30,119)      40 x 25 mm tunnel     (SoC ~ mid-board)     (open)
```

* Fan sits on the lid directly above the intake slots, blowing **horizontally** (+x) into the duct, through the Pi's heatsink, and out of the exhaust slots beyond the board edge. Air enters from underneath the case, so the box is not sucking from the user's stage-side face.
* The case rests on its rubber feet (≥ 6 mm clearance, so the slots in the lid are not sealed against the floor). Do not put the pedal on carpet with the feet removed.
* Duct: 3D-printed in PETG or ASA, 1.6 mm walls, cross-section 40 × 25 mm, length ≈ 70 mm, with a rounded exit over the heatsink. Print files are not part of this card; the dimensions above are the spec. Fan shroud fits M3 × 20 mm screws into the lid tapped holes at fan corners (32 mm hole spacing).
* **Filter:** 5 mm open-cell foam (PPI 30) behind the intake slots, replaceable. Clean every 6 months.
* **Nothing mounted in the airflow tunnel**: cables are tied along the walls; the buck module sits behind the display, outside the tunnel but in the box air, so its 1.5 W only warms the intake air a little.

### 5.4 Fan failure and passive fallback

Without the fan, the same sink in still air is ≈ 8 K/W: the die would go 40 + 9 × 9.4 ≈ 125 °C. That is not tolerable, so the plan is:

1. **Case coupling (partial, optional):** a 1.5 mm 6 W/mK gap pad from the heatsink top to the inside of the top panel, if the display layout leaves a clear gap there, lets some heat reach the 188 × 188 × 67 mm die-cast shell (≈ 0.8 W/K in free air). That holds a limp-home SoC load of roughly 4–5 W, not 9 W. It is a bonus, not part of the design margin. Not calculated precisely: measure it.
2. **Software fallback:** the engine health monitor raises an alarm at 75 °C and the pedal UI shows "fan?" It must never silently throttle mid-gig. The Pi hits soft throttle at 80 °C and caps the clocks; that protects the hardware, but the NAM model may xrun. This is documented as a failure mode, not a design target.
3. **Fan status:** the Pi 5 fan header gives a tach. If the tach is zero while the fan is requested, `pedal-health` should log "fan stalled". (Follow-up for t_3f42020c; not part of this card.)

The hard limit is 85 °C with the SoC's own protection above that; this design relies on the fan for normal operation and on the Pi's firmware for fault protection. It does **not** rely on passive cooling alone.

### 5.5 Fan control

Fan on the Pi 5's 4-pin PWM header (5 V, 1.0 mm pitch JST-SH). The firmware curve (set in `/boot/firmware/config.txt`) is:

```
dtparam=fan_temp0=45000
dtparam=fan_temp0_speed=90
dtparam=fan_temp1=55000
dtparam=fan_temp1_speed=140
dtparam=fan_temp2=65000
dtparam=fan_temp2_speed=200
dtparam=fan_temp3=72000
dtparam=fan_temp3_speed=255
```

(`speed` is 0–255 PWM duty; verify the parameter names against the current Raspberry Pi OS docs.) This is more aggressive than the Pi 5 default (50/60/67.5/75 °C) because the design wants the fan on before the sink saturates. Audio noise: fan at low duty is quiet; a stage covers it. Fan and 5 V wires pass through a ferrite bead, and the fan runs from the Pi header (already on the filtered 5 V rail), not a separate supply.

## 7. Assembly

1. Print the SVGs at 100 %, check with a ruler (a 100 mm scale line is not drawn; measure the 188 mm outline). Tape to the panel, centre-punch every crosshair.
2. Drill and file the **top panel**: 2 × 12.2, 3 × 8, OLED and display windows (drill corners, nibble/jigsaw, file to the line; leave 0.2 mm for sanding). Walls: 9.5/12 mm holes. Lid: slots (drill the two end holes, file the web) and Pi/feet holes.
3. De-burr, wash, optionally paint or powder-coat **before** fitting parts. Mask the inside of the DC and jack holes for ground continuity if needed.
4. Fit jacks, DC, USB panel, footswitches, LEDs. Wire the star ground (`power.md` §3.6): one ground point at the DC jack sleeve.
5. Epoxy or screw four M3 blind studs behind the display window (spacing from the display module's mounting lugs); fit the display with 2 mm foam.
6. Stick the heatsink on the SoC. Fit the Pi on the stand-offs on the lid (screw from below). Plug fan, USB, power leads (USB-C data-free cable from the buck, `power.md` §3.4).
7. Fit the fan and duct, then the filter foam. Close the lid (do not pinch the cable bundle).
8. Power up the bench supply **with the lid off first**: check 5 V at the Pi with a meter (`power.md` §8), check the fan spins at boot, and run the health monitor. Fix before closing.

## 8. Verification (owned by `t_e77e98f1`)

* 60-minute run, heaviest chain plus `stress-ng --cpu 4`, in a 25 °C room and, as a hot case, with the case wrapped in a loosely fitting cardboard sleeve to emulate ~40 °C.
* Log `vcgencmd measure_temp` and `get_throttled` (the `pedal-health` crate). Pass: peak < 80 °C, `get_throttled` = 0x0 throughout.
* Block the fan for 30 s: expect the die to pass 80 °C and the firmware to throttle; confirm no crash and the UI warning shows.
* If the room measurement exceeds **65 °C** at 25 °C ambient, then at 40 °C the model's 70 °C prediction is wrong; enlarge the heatsink or add a second slot pair.

## 9. Open items

* 12 V vs 5 V fan (§0).
* Exact USB-A panel part and display module outline.
